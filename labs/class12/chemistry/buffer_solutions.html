Theory — Henderson–Hasselbalch equation

An acidic buffer of acetic acid (HA) and acetate (A⁻) resists pH change. For total concentration C = 0.10 M in 50 mL, the ratio r = [A⁻]/[HA] sets the pH:

pH = pKₐ + log₁₀([A⁻] / [HA])    with pKₐ (acetate) = 4.76

Each drop = 0.05 mL of 1.0 M strong acid (HCl, drops < 0) or strong alkali (NaOH, drops > 0). Added H⁺ converts A⁻ → HA; added OH⁻ converts HA → A⁻. Total volume Vtot = 50 mL + |drops| × 0.05 mL gives a small dilution correction; it cancels in the ratio while buffer survives, and sets the pH of excess H⁺/OH⁻ once the buffer is exhausted. The pH-vs-drops curve is flat near the centre — the buffer region.

Precautions & Standard Errors

  • Calibrate the pH meter with standard buffers (pH 4.00 and 7.00) before use.
  • Rinse the electrode with distilled water between readings; blot, do not wipe.
  • Stir gently and wait for a stable reading; control temperature near 25 °C.
  • Measure drops uniformly; record the ratio before adding acid/alkali.
