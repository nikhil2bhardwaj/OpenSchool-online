Theory

Ethanol undergoes controlled oxidation to ethanal (acetaldehyde) and vigorous oxidation to ethanoic acid (acetic acid). Strong oxidants in acidic/alkaline medium drive over-oxidation; heated copper gives controlled dehydrogenation stopping at the aldehyde.

CH₃CH₂OH + [O] → CH₃CHO + H₂O   →   CH₃COOH (vigorous [O])
  • Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇: orange Cr(VI) → green Cr(III). Warm gently: ethanal first; on heating gives ethanoic acid.
  • Alkaline KMnO₄: purple MnO₄⁻ → brown MnO₂ precipitate. Product isolated as ethanoate; acidification gives ethanoic acid.
  • Heated Cu (CuO/Cu): black CuO-coated wire turns pink-brown Cu; ethanol vapours dehydrogenate to ethanal only (no over-oxidation).
  • Distinguishing tests: Tollens' (silver mirror) positive for ethanal, negative for ethanoic acid; NaHCO₃ effervescence / blue litmus→red positive for acid only; iodoform positive for ethanol/ethanal (CH₃CO– group precursor), negative for ethanoic acid.

Precautions & Standard Errors

  • Warm gently in a water bath — never boil ethanol directly over a flame (flammable).
  • Use dilute solutions; add oxidant dropwise to control over-oxidation.
  • For the Cu method, pass vapours over freshly heated CuO wire; reheat wire between passes.
  • Smell products cautiously (wafting); do not inhale vapours directly.
  • Record colour change against a white background for consistent end-point judgement.

Triage

Toggle “Overheat” to observe forced over-oxidation: ethanal readings shift to ethanoic acid and Tollens' goes negative.
