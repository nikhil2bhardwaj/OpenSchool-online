Theory

Persulphate oxidises iodide slowly: S₂O₈²⁻ + 2I⁻ → 2SO₄²⁻ + I₂. Added thiosulphate instantly reduces each trace of I₂ back to I⁻ (I₂ + 2S₂O₃²⁻ → 2I⁻ + S₄O₆²⁻), so no free iodine accumulates. When the fixed thiosulphate dose is exhausted, liberated iodine suddenly forms the deep-blue starch–iodine complex — the "clock" signal.

rate = k·[S₂O₈²⁻]m·[I⁻]n,  m = 1,  n = 1
k(T) = A·e−Ea/RT,  A = 1.2×10⁷ M⁻¹s⁻¹,  Ea = 52 kJ·mol⁻¹
tblue = [S₂O₃²⁻]₀ consumed / rate,  [S₂O₃²⁻]₀ consumed = 0.0010 M

Since 1/tblue ∝ rate, plotting 1/t versus [S₂O₈²⁻] at fixed [I⁻] gives a straight line through the origin whose slope ∝ k. Comparing runs isolates orders m and n; repeats at several temperatures show k rising per Arrhenius. The canvas shows the 1/t vs [S₂O₈²⁻] line plus a beaker preview that turns blue at the clock time.

Precautions & Standard Errors

  • Start the stopwatch at the instant of mixing; add starch indicator at the same stage every run.
  • Measure all volumes with the same graduated pipettes; keep total volume constant so concentrations are comparable.
  • Maintain constant temperature in a water bath; rinse glassware to remove traces of iodine or thiosulphate.
  • Judge the blue end-point against the same white background and record several repeats.
