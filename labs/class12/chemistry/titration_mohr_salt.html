Theory

2 KMnO₄ + 8 H₂SO₄ + 10 FeSO₄(NH₄)₂SO₄·6H₂O → K₂SO₄ + 2 MnSO₄ + 5 Fe₂(SO₄)₃ + 10 (NH₄)₂SO₄ + 68 H₂O

Mn⁷⁺ is reduced to Mn²⁺ (5-electron change) while Fe²⁺ oxidises to Fe³⁺ (1-electron change). Hence 1 mol KMnO₄ ≡ 5 mol Fe²⁺ and

M₁V₁ = (2/10) · M₂V₂   ⟹   M₁ = (2/10)·(M₂V₂)/V₁

KMnO₄ is its own indicator: the first excess drop in acid medium gives a permanent faint pink end-point.

Precautions & Standard Errors

  • Acidify with dilute H₂SO₄ only — never HCl (Cl⁻ is oxidised) or HNO₃ (itself an oxidiser).
  • Read the upper meniscus of the dark KMnO₄ solution.
  • Add the last drops very slowly and swirl after each drop near the end-point.
  • Do not use a rubber stop, and avoid parallax while reading the burette.
  • Take at least three concordant readings (within 0.1 mL).

Triage faults

Omit H₂SO₄: brown MnO₂ sludge forms instead of a pink end-point. Over-titration: excess KMnO₄ turns the solution dark purple and invalidates the trial.
