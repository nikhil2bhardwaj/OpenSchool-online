NCERT Systematic Scheme

1 · Dry Tests

  • Colour and smell of the salt.
  • Action of heat / flame test.
  • Solubility in water.

2 · Acid Radicals (Anions)

  • Dilute H₂SO₄ & conc. H₂SO₄ tests.
  • BaCl₂ for sulfate; AgNO₃ for chloride.
  • Brown ring for nitrate; Nessler's for ammonium.

3 · Basic Radicals (Cations)

  • Zero group — NH₄⁺ (NaOH / Nessler's).
  • Group I — Pb²⁺ · Group II — Cu²⁺.
  • Group III — Fe³⁺/Al³⁺ · Group IV — Zn²⁺.
Group reagents: dil. HCl → Group I · H₂S/HCl → Group II · NH₄OH/NH₄Cl → Group III · H₂S/NH₄OH → Group IV

Precautions

  • Use freshly prepared reagents and clean test tubes for each test.
  • Heat the test tube gently, holding it with a holder and pointing it away from the body.
  • Do not smell gases directly; waft the vapours towards your nose.
  • Acidify the salt solution before testing with BaCl₂ and AgNO₃ to avoid false positives from carbonate.
  • Confirm every acid radical with a confirmatory test before concluding.

Triage faults

Un-acidified BaCl₂/AgNO₃ can give a false white precipitate with a carbonate; over-heating destroys the confirmatory colour; impure reagents give misleading flame colours.
