Theory

A compound microscope uses a short-focus objective to form a real, inverted, magnified intermediate image inside the tube, which the eyepiece then magnifies again as a simple magnifier with the final image at the near point (D = 25 cm).

M = Mo · Me = −(L / fo) · (D / fe),   D = 25 cm

L is the tube length (distance between objective and intermediate image), fo the objective focal length and fe the eyepiece focal length. The negative sign denotes inversion. Shorter fo and fe, and a longer tube, all increase magnification. Ray diagram on the stage shows: object arrow → objective lens → intermediate image arrow (inverted, magnified) → eyepiece lens → final magnified image arrow.

Precautions & Standard Errors

  • Focus the objective first, then the eyepiece; never force coarse focus near the slide.
  • Keep lenses clean and centred on the same optical axis; avoid parallax while reading scales.
  • Use uniform illumination and record fo, fe, L with correct units (cm).
  • Take multiple readings and use the mean magnification to reduce random error.
