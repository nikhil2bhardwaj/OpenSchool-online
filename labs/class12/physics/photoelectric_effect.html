Theory

Einstein's photoelectric equation: maximum kinetic energy of emitted photoelectrons is KEmax = h·f − φ, where h = 4.1357×10⁻¹⁵ eV·s is Planck's constant, f is the incident light frequency and φ is the cathode work function. Emission occurs only when h·f ≥ φ. The stopping potential Vs (in volts) numerically equals KEmax (in eV).

KEmax = h·f − φ   ·   f₀ = φ / h   ·   e·Vs = KEmax

Threshold frequencies: Cs (2.1 eV) → ≈508 THz · Zn (4.3 eV) → ≈1040 THz · Pt (5.6 eV) → ≈1354 THz. The canvas plots KEmax vs f for the selected cathode; the dashed line marks f₀.

Precautions & Standard Errors

  • Keep the photocell free of stray light; zero the voltmeter before each set.
  • Use monochromatic light and allow the source intensity to stabilise.
  • Record stopping potential at true zero photocurrent; repeat and average.
