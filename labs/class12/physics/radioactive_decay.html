Theory

Radioactive decay is a first-order statistical process: the number of undecayed nuclei decreases exponentially with time. This sandbox plots the expected-value (mean) curve, not a random Monte-Carlo trial, so every reading is exactly reproducible.

N(t) = N₀ · e^(−λt)    with    λ = ln 2 / T½    (N₀ = 1000 nuclei)

Half-life T½ is the time in which half the nuclei decay. Activity A = λN is the decay rate. After n half-lives, N = N₀ / 2ⁿ. A semi-log plot of ln N vs t is a straight line of slope −λ.

Precautions & Standard Errors

  • Subtract background counts (cosmic rays, lab sources) from every reading before fitting λ or T½.
  • Avoid parallax while reading scaler/timer scales; keep the eye perpendicular.
  • Use the same counting interval and geometry for all readings; record dead-time corrections if applicable.
  • Take multiple readings and use the mean to reduce random error.
