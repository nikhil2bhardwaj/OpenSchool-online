Theory

Pollen grains germinate in a sugar solution kept in a humid cavity slide. Sucrose maintains osmotic balance and supplies energy. Germination percentage rises with incubation time and is maximum near an optimum sucrose concentration (~10–15%), falling on either side. Pollen-tube length grows approximately linearly with time once germination begins.

germ % = 96 · exp(−(C−12)² / 72) · T / (T+30)   |   tube = 0.9 · exp(−(C−12)² / 100) · T

C = sucrose %, T = incubation minutes. Count germinated grains (tube longer than grain diameter) in several microscope fields and compute percentage.

Precautions & Standard Errors

  • Prepare fresh sucrose solutions accurately; label each cavity slide.
  • Keep slides in a moist Petri dish (covered) so drops do not dry out.
  • Use the same flower / anther maturity and count at least 100 grains per reading.
  • A dry or stale slide (fault) systematically lowers germination — discard such preparations.
