Theory

For small angular displacement the pendulum obeys simple harmonic motion and

T = 2π √(L_eff / g)  ⟹  g = 4π² (L_eff / T²)

The simulation integrates the exact nonlinear equation below with a fourth-order Runge–Kutta scheme, so large amplitudes show the real period dilation that the small-angle formula ignores:

d²θ/dt² + (g/L)·sin θ + b·(dθ/dt) = 0

Precautions & Standard Errors

  • Keep the amplitude small (below ~10°) so the small-angle approximation holds.
  • Use a spherical bob so L is measured from the point of suspension to the centre of the bob.
  • Count oscillations from the mean position and use the split-cork clamp for a firm, fixed suspension point.
  • Measure the diameter with a vernier caliper and apply the zero-error correction.
  • Time a large number of oscillations (e.g. 20) to reduce the human reaction-time error.

Period dilation

Increase the amplitude above 10° and watch T increase, making the computed g fall below 9.81 m/s².
